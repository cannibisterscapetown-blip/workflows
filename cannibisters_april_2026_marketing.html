Deep Dive

The Correction Email That Broke Records

A date mistake turned into April's highest-performing campaign — the accidental best email of the quarter.

What Happened

The 4/20 Carnival announcement sent that morning (8am) stated the wrong day. When a correction was sent at 6:29PM the same day, urgency and FOMO kicked in — subscribers who had just read the announcement email were primed and already thinking about the event.

The correction email achieved a 32.3% open rate — the highest of any April campaign and significantly above the 22% average for the loyal segments. Importantly, it drove R107,289 in attributed revenue — nearly 3× the original carnival announcement email (R32,337). The subject line "Correction: Our 4/20 Carnival is on Monday (not Sunday)" triggered curiosity-open behaviour at scale.

Takeaway: Urgency-framed subject lines drive opens

A correction/update email pattern creates genuine curiosity that bypasses inbox fatigue. Consider testing "Update:" or "Important:" subject lines for future high-stakes campaigns — intentionally, not accidentally. The FOMO multiplier effect (two emails same day, same event) also doubled the touchpoints without feeling spammy for this audience.

A/B Conclusion: Personalisation didn't move the needle here

Both variations landed near-identically at ~8.5% open rate and ~2.4% CTOR on the lapsed 90-day segment. The lapsed audience is harder to re-engage regardless of subject line framing. Worth A/B testing the actual offer (membership vs. discount code) rather than just the personalisation. Future consideration: segment lapsed 90-day vs. lapsed 180-day — they likely respond very differently.

Bounce alert: Re-engagement list has significant deliverability issues

1,396 bounces on 12,709 recipients = 11% bounce rate. The lapsed segment naturally contains stale email addresses. Consider running a list cleaning pass — removing hard bounces from this segment before the next send to protect sender reputation.

Key Insights

What April Taught Us

Observations, patterns and action items from April 2026 email performance.

1. The accidental correction email formula works

Subject lines framed as corrections or updates generate urgency-opens. 32.3% open rate vs 22% average. Consider intentionally engineering "update" emails for important campaigns.

2. Post-event emails stay high-performing

The "Thank You for Coming" email (Apr 21) achieved 28.5% opens and R66,680 revenue — proving that momentum continues after an event. The audience is warm, engaged and still buying.

3. Abandoned Checkout is the highest-efficiency touchpoint

R89.31 revenue per recipient vs R4–15 for campaigns. 5.25% conversion rate. Every rand invested in optimising this flow returns 6–20× more than broadcast campaigns.

4. Re-engagement campaigns need offer testing, not subject testing

Both A/B variations opened identically. The bottleneck is the offer (free membership) — not the personalisation. A direct discount code may work better for truly lapsed customers.

5. List hygiene is urgent on the full Email List

91.3% delivery on 14,786 sends = ~1,285 hard bounces. A quarterly list clean pass on the full list would protect sender reputation and improve campaign performance metrics immediately.

6. Click rates are uniformly low across all campaigns

CTR ranges from 0.18%–0.49%. Opens are strong, but subscribers aren't clicking through to the site. Single large CTA buttons with more compelling copy or urgency could push click rates meaningfully higher.

7. April had no SMS campaigns — an untapped channel

All 8 sends were email-only. SMS campaigns, particularly for same-day event reminders like the 4/20 Carnival, would likely generate significantly higher open rates (SMS open rates average 90%+) and could drive in-store traffic on the day.

8. The metric-based flow needs investigation

485 recipients, 0 conversions, 4.5% open rate. This flow isn't working — either the trigger, timing or messaging needs a full rebuild before it can contribute to revenue.
